My interests as a kid were pretty standard boy interests. I liked video games
and sports. It was not the specifics of a single game or sport that hooked me,
but rather the patterns that are fundamental to games. Every game, and every
sport, are a system in which you can act, receive feedback, and improve
upon. The draw was understanding the system, mastering it, and becoming creative
within it.

I cannot ignore the social element that contributed to my enjoyment to these
activities. Even though I played a lot of video games by myself, there was
almost always a family member or friend that was playing the same game as
me. This resulted in text messages and Xbox party chats where we would talk
about our experiences playing the game. The social aspect of sports is
obvious. It is important to recognize the role of these relationships in your
satisfaction with activities. I mistakingly thought I could love video games to
the same degree without the social element. In reality, the shared pursuit is
what makes it special.

Games take aspects from life as a whole, and package them in a
sandbox. Interaction with games mimic our interaction with life. They provide us
rules that we can understand and learn within without becoming overwhelemed by
uncertainty. The universe does not promise the same thing. It does however give
us clues through observable patterns. These patterns are not always easy to see,
as we become clouded with emotion and a desire for certainty. Religion and
philosophies attempt to capture these patterns in a more comprehensible system.

I think that if we acknowledge that games are not superficial, but are actually
quite capable teacheers, we can learn a lot about ourselves and our place in the
world at large. Extrapolate your behavior in games to have a deeper
understanding of how you show up in other aspects of life. When I play games, I
want to strategize and organize the system perfectly. I want mastery, and I will
rush to get there. I do not want to linger in the training grounds, I want to
get to the good part, which is the middle of the story. In the middle, at the
peak, you have the confidence in your abilities which enables you to be creative
and enjoy the challenges. I feel the same way about relationships.

For me, the best part of relationships is not the excitement and potential of
something new. The best part is when you have the foundation of trust and
understanding. When both parties are bought in to that stable foundation, you
can then become creative with the relationship dynamics. You become free to
explore, grow, and challenge each other. What can be found here is so much more
profound than the excitement felt at the beginning. Unfortunately, I have a
tendency to rush through the beginning. My eye is on the bigger picture, not the
fleeting rush of excitement. This does not present itself in the form of 'love
bombing', but rather anxiety and impatience. I want to spend as much time in the
middle as possible. Depth over breadth, quality over quantity.

A relationship is a system, and a game is a system. The universe exposes the
same patterns within all systems, across all domains. When I learn something in
a video game, software engineering, or relationship, I can apply it to other
areas of my life. Successful systems tend to have strong foundations. These
foundations are built through consistency, where feedback can be received,
direction can be adapted, and consistency can continue. The end result is what
we see, and what we want, but you have to ask what foundation it was built
upon. On a personal level, my biggest challenge is learning how to enjoy the
building of the foundation, and accepting that it might be a failed project.
